what fashion week taught me about being in rooms you don't belong in

Operations Assistant · Fashion Tech at NYFW

I went to New York Fashion Week with zero fashion credentials and a coat check clipboard. I was there as an operations assistant for a fashion tech company — which in practice meant running logistics, managing the door, working behind the bar, and doing whatever needed to happen in the hour before each show. It was glamorous in the way all backstage things are: chaotic, fluorescent, held together by people who are very good at looking calm.

What struck me immediately was how much of it runs on relationship infrastructure. Who you know, who you nod at, who you can pull into a corner conversation. The tech world has this too, but it's less visible. Here it was the whole game, played in real time, in a room where everyone was watching everyone else.

The people I met didn't fit any one category. A VC scouting for the next wave of fashion tech investments. Someone building a startup to make fast fashion more transparent, tracking garments from factory to shelf. A founder demoing a smart mirror that let you try on outfits without changing clothes. I kept expecting hard walls between industries and kept not finding them.
