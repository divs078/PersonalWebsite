rock climbing and the slow business of building confidence

Rock Climbing · Adventure

I didn't get into climbing for any deep reason — I was interning in Atlanta, everyone I knew was scattered somewhere else, and I needed something to do. But it turned out to be one of those things where the more you do it, the more it has to say. Every route is a problem. The holds are there, the sequence isn't, and you figure it out with your whole body or you don't. You can't fake strength you haven't built yet.

What I didn't expect was how gradually your relationship with fear changes. There are moves I wouldn't have attempted when I started — reaches that felt too far, jumps that felt too committing. Now I'll look at something that would've scared me six months ago and just go for it. Not because the fear is gone, but because at some point you've fallen enough times that the falling feels less catastrophic. You build up enough evidence that you can figure it out, and then you just kind of do.

Some of the best friends I've made were through climbing — people who'd cheer you through a move you'd been stuck on for weeks, who'd quietly point out a beta without making you feel like you needed it, who made the space feel genuinely supportive in a way that's rarer than it should be. We kept showing up to the same wall, and they stayed because they were the kind of people worth staying for.
