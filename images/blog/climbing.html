rock climbing and the slow business of building confidence

Rock Climbing · Adventure

Rock climbing is a sport that keeps an honest record of you. You can't fake strength you haven't built. Every route is a problem — the holds are there, the sequence isn't — and you figure it out with your whole body, or you don't.

What I didn't expect was how gradually your relationship with fear changes. There are moves I wouldn't have attempted when I started — reaches that felt too far, jumps that felt too committing. Now I'll look at something that would've scared me six months ago and just... go for it. Not because the fear is gone, but because somewhere along the way you build enough trust in yourself to take the jump and see if you've gotten strong enough to hold on. Sometimes you have. Sometimes you haven't and you fall and you try again. That's kind of the whole thing.

I started climbing while interning in Atlanta — everyone I knew was scattered across New York, the Bay Area, wherever — so I was looking for something to do and people to do it with. The climbing gym filled both. Some of the best friends I've made were through it — people who'd cheer you through a move you'd been stuck on for weeks, who'd quietly point out a beta without making you feel like you needed it, who made the space feel genuinely supportive in a way that's rarer than it should be. I met them because we kept showing up to the same wall, and they stayed because they were the kind of people worth staying for.
