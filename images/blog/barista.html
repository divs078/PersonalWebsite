learning to pull espresso, one terrible shot at a time

Barista Training · Life

I started learning to make coffee because I wanted to do something that required my hands and not my brain. What I didn't expect was how much there is to find inside a motion you do a hundred times a day.

The repetition is the whole thing. Same grind, same tamp, same pull — over and over until your hands just know. There's something quietly satisfying about that kind of learning. It doesn't live in your head. It accumulates in your wrists, your grip, the way you level the portafilter without thinking about it. You stop deciding and start doing, and that shift feels like something.

Once the basics are in your body, you start noticing inefficiency everywhere. The extra step between the grinder and the group head. The way you're reaching when you could be pivoting. Small things that add up across a hundred drinks. I started optimizing not because anyone asked me to, but because once you're doing the same motion that many times, waste becomes obvious. It was the first time I understood ergonomics as something you feel rather than something you read about.

The interactions surprised me most. Handing someone their drink first thing in the morning — made right, handed over without fumbling — is a small thing that lands. You can tell. People soften a little. It takes thirty seconds and it's genuinely good for them. Most of those moments disappear unnoticed, but being on the making side of it made me start catching them more on the receiving end too. You notice when someone got it right for you.

I'm still not great at latte art. But everything else got quieter in a way I needed.
