learning to pull espresso, one terrible shot at a time

Barista Training · Life

I started learning to make coffee because I wanted something that required my hands and not my brain.

The repetition is the point. Same grind, same tamp, same pull — until your hands just know. It doesn't live in your head, it builds up in your grip. You stop thinking about the motion and start noticing everything around it.

That's when the inefficiency becomes obvious. The extra step between grinder and group head. The reach you're doing when you could pivot. I started optimizing not because anyone asked, but because waste is hard to ignore once you're doing the same thing a hundred times.

Handing someone a drink that's actually right — you can tell it lands. People soften a little. Thirty seconds, and it matters more than you'd expect. I hadn't been on that side of it before.
