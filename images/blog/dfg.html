Dec 2023 – Mar 2024

building a child safety app in nigeria — and using your skills for good

Develop for Good · Product Manager

I joined Develop for Good because I wanted to do more than build a product — I wanted to really think about the people who would use it. Our client was a nonprofit in Nigeria working on child safety, and the most important lesson I learned was that the user is not an abstract persona. They are a real person with a time zone, a language, and cultural expectations that shape every interaction.

We were separated by a big time difference, so most of our conversations happened asynchronously. That meant I couldn't just rely on quick back-and-forth feedback. I had to slow down, listen harder, and translate what the user needed into product decisions that made sense in their context.

The language barrier showed up not only in words, but in assumptions. A question that sounded simple to us in English could mean something very different in a different cultural context. The team spent extra time learning how people in the community actually talked about safety, trust, and reporting — and then we designed flows that honored that language instead of forcing ours onto them.

There were also cultural customs to think about. In some places, the act of reporting abuse is deeply personal and carries social risk. That changed how we approached notifications, privacy, and onboarding. Our app had to feel safe and respectful before it could feel useful.

This project taught me that building good product is as much about empathy as it is about engineering. I became a better product manager by asking myself: am I really thinking about the user as they are, not as the user I wish they were? That mindset made the work feel less like a feature checklist and more like a responsibility.
